# Porchlight Club — First Video Batch

Created 2026-08-10. Channel: `youtube.com/@Porchlight_Club` — TMT's top-of-funnel arm.
Audience is TMT's audience: owner-led contractors and service businesses, 0–15 employees,
St. Petersburg / Tampa / Pinellas / Hillsborough.

**The structural point:** each video has one matching page on the site. The video answers the
question; the page is where someone goes when they want it applied to their own shop. Without that
pairing, views are just views.

**Rules that apply to every video below**
- No invented numbers, no client names, no results you cannot show. If a number would help,
  say "count it this week and see" instead of making one up.
- Voice: short sentences, contractor vocabulary — calls, trucks, board, tickets, callbacks,
  drive time, close-out. Banned: leverage, streamline, solutions, seamless, unlock, robust.
- Every video ends with one free thing to do this week.
- Say the vendor position out loud once per video. It is the differentiator.
- Shoot in a shop, a bay, or a truck. Not at a desk with a bookshelf.

## Pillar 1 — "What should a small business automate first?"

**Maps to:** `/insights/what-to-automate-first` → `/ai-automation`
**Length:** 7–9 min

**Title:** What Should You Automate First in a Contracting Business?
**Hook (first 5 seconds, before any intro):**
> "Most shops automate the most annoying job first. That is almost never the one costing you money."

**Beats**
1. **The scene (0:00–0:45).** The most annoying task and the most expensive task are two different
   things. Only one of them pays you back.
2. **The test (0:45–3:00).** Three questions, on screen as you say them:
   does it repeat daily · are the steps identical every time · does it cost money when skipped.
   A task needs all three. Two is not enough.
3. **The order (3:00–6:30).** Missed calls first. Estimate follow-up second. Field notes to invoice
   third. Explain *why* missed calls are first: a missed call leaves no evidence, so nobody in the
   business ever sees it.
4. **What not to automate (6:30–8:00).** Anything reaching a customer unread. Pricing. Safety and
   code. A process nobody can describe out loud. Be blunt here — this is the trust-building part.
5. **Close (8:00–8:30).** Count your missed calls for five working days. Just the number.

**On-screen text:** the three test questions · the four-step order · "count 5 days"
**CTA:** "The written version, with the full list, is on the site — link below."
**Description first line:** Automate the work that repeats daily, follows the same steps every
time, and costs you money when it gets forgotten. Here is the order that usually holds.

## Pillar 2 — "How to choose a field service CRM without getting trapped"

**Maps to:** `/insights/choose-a-field-service-crm` → `/crm-workflow-consulting`
**Length:** 9–12 min. This is the money video — highest commercial intent on the channel.

**Title:** 6 Questions to Ask Before You Buy Field Service Software
**Hook:**
> "The demo already has a finished pricebook, clean customer records, and a dispatcher who knows
> the system. You have none of that on day one. That gap is where shops lose the money."

**Beats**
1. **The gap (0:00–1:30).** Demo state vs. your month one. Name it plainly.
2. **The six questions (1:30–8:00).** One chapter marker each — YouTube chapters matter here
   because people re-watch this one before a sales call:
   - What does setup actually require *from us*?
   - What happens to my data if I leave?
   - What is the real cost at my size, in year two?
   - Which parts of this demo are add-ons?
   - Who at my company has to change how they work?
   - Can I talk to a shop my size that uses this?
3. **The pricebook warning (8:00–9:30).** The one that ambushes people: if you have never had a
   written pricebook, setup is not data entry — it is hundreds of first-time pricing decisions
   made while still running calls. This is why go-live dates slip by months.
4. **Matching platform to shop (9:30–11:00).** Heavy platforms repay setup only if a pricebook,
   a dispatcher and consistent close-out already exist. If nobody owns the board, fix that first.
5. **Close (11:00–11:30).** Before the next demo, write down three things: who builds tomorrow's
   schedule, where prices come from, who chases estimates that did not close.

**On-screen text:** all six questions as chapter cards · "ask on a call, not by email — the
hesitations are informative"
**CTA:** "Print the six questions from the link below and take them into your next demo."

## Pillar 3 — "What AI consulting actually looks like in St. Pete"

**Maps to:** `/insights/ai-consulting-st-petersburg-what-to-expect` → `/ai-consulting-st-petersburg-fl`
**Length:** 6–8 min. This is the local-intent video — the one that should rank for the market.

**Title:** What to Expect From an AI Consultant (St. Petersburg & Tampa Bay)
**Hook:**
> "If somebody names a software tool in the first meeting, before they have seen how your shop
> runs, you are in a sales process. Not a consulting one."

**Beats**
1. **The tell (0:00–1:00).** Recommendation before diagnosis. Cover it first — it is the most
   useful thing in the video and it earns the rest.
2. **What the visit is (1:00–3:00).** On site, 45–60 minutes, free, no obligation. Why on site:
   the whiteboard that overrides the software, the sticky notes on the dispatcher's monitor, the
   tech who texts instead of closing the job. None of that shows on a video call.
3. **The six questions asked (3:00–5:00).** Board ownership · close-out · where pricing comes from
   · missed calls · estimate follow-up · who owns it after go-live.
4. **What is local (5:00–6:30).** Pinellas drive time deciding calls-per-truck. Summer peak and
   storm surge breaking manual systems first — work done in spring is worth several times the same
   work in August. Two sets of permitting if you run both sides of the bay.
5. **Close (6:30–7:00).** Write down the two or three things you most want fixed, in your own
   words, before you call anyone.

**CTA:** "Shop visit is free and I come to you — Pinellas and Hillsborough. Link below."

## YouTube setup that actually matters

- **Channel description** must say what you do and where: AI consulting, automation, CRM and
  workflow guidance for contractors in St. Petersburg, Tampa and Pinellas County. Link to the site.
- **Chapters on Pillar 2.** People re-watch that one in pieces.
- **First line of every description** is the answer, not a greeting. It is what gets indexed.
- **Link the matching page in the first two lines**, above the fold — not buried under socials.
- **Pinned comment** = the free action from the video. Highest-engagement real estate on the page.
- Consistency beats production value at this stage. One good pillar a month, shorts in between.

## Sequencing

Publish Pillar 3 first. It is the shortest, it carries the local intent, and it explains the
offer — so the other two have somewhere to send people. Then Pillar 2 (commercial intent), then
Pillar 1 (broadest reach, weakest intent).

## What is deliberately not here

No testimonials, no case studies, no before/after numbers, no client footage. Those come after
real engagements with permission. Publishing them early is the fastest way to lose the credibility
the rest of this is built on.
