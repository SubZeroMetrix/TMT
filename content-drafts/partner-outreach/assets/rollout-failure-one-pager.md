# Why Field Service Software Rollouts Fail in Small Shops

**Six questions to ask an owner before they turn anything on — and what each answer predicts.**

Modern Trades Mentor · Richard Fritzke · 727-600-3425 · themoderntradesmentor.com

---

Software almost never fails on its own. It fails because a shop bought a new way of working and
only installed the app. The crew works around it, the office keeps the old spreadsheet running in
parallel, and five months later the owner says the software didn't work.

Every one of these six questions is about a process, not a feature. Ask them before go-live. If
three or more come back weak, fix the process first — the rollout will fail otherwise, and the
shop will blame the product.

---

### 1. Who builds tomorrow's board, and what are they looking at when they do it?

**Good answer:** one named person, at a set time, working from one place.
**Weak answer:** "It depends," or two people who both think they own it, or a whiteboard plus
a phone plus somebody's memory.

**What it predicts:** dispatch is the center of gravity for every FSM product. If nobody owns the
board today, the software becomes a fourth place to look instead of the only place. This is the
single most common reason a rollout stalls.

---

### 2. When a tech finishes a job, how does the office find out?

**Good answer:** the tech does one thing, at the truck, before pulling away.
**Weak answer:** a text, a call at the end of the day, or paperwork dropped off Friday.

**What it predicts:** if closing out a job is currently a favor the tech does when they remember,
adding required fields to a phone app makes it slower, not faster. Techs will batch it to the end
of the week and the data will be wrong. Fix the close-out habit before go-live, on paper if you
have to.

---

### 3. Where does the price come from?

**Good answer:** a written pricebook everyone uses, even if it lives in a binder.
**Weak answer:** "the guys know," or each tech prices their own work, or it's in the owner's head.

**What it predicts:** this is the quiet rollout killer. Building a pricebook is the largest piece
of setup work in any of these products, and a shop that has never had one is not doing data entry
— they're making hundreds of pricing decisions for the first time, mid-rollout, while running
calls. Budget for it separately or the go-live date slips by months.

---

### 4. What happens to a call nobody answers?

**Good answer:** somebody checks and calls back, on a schedule, and it's written down.
**Weak answer:** voicemail, or "we usually catch it."

**What it predicts:** how much the software is actually going to be worth. Missed calls are the
clearest money leak in a small shop, and a shop that can't answer this question won't be able to
tell whether the software helped. No baseline, no proof, no renewal.

---

### 5. Who chases the estimates that didn't close?

**Good answer:** a named person with a set day.
**Weak answer:** nobody, or "the owner when he gets a minute."

**What it predicts:** follow-up is where these products pay for themselves, and it's the first
feature to go unused. If nobody owns follow-up on paper today, no automation will create the
habit — it will just send messages nobody reads the replies to.

---

### 6. Who owns this after go-live?

**Good answer:** one person, by name, with time protected for it.
**Weak answer:** "all of us," or the owner on top of a full week.

**What it predicts:** whether any of the above sticks. A rollout with no internal owner reverts
inside 90 days. This person doesn't have to be technical. They have to be the one who notices
when somebody stops using it, and says something.

---

## Reading the results

| Weak answers | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Go. Set the date. |
| 2–3 | Fix those processes first. Two to four weeks of work, most of it free. |
| 4+ | Don't turn anything on yet. The software will get blamed for a problem it can't fix. |

---

## One thing to do this week, that costs nothing

Pick question 1. Name one person who owns tomorrow's board, tell the crew who it is, and have
them build it from one place for five straight days. If that alone doesn't hold for a week, no
software was going to fix it.
